Core Research Thesis

Generative AI generates executable code in seconds, but unconstrained access fosters severe cognitive offloading and epistemic dependence. This research formulates a real-time, telemetry-driven composite metric: the AI Over-Reliance Index (ORI). Through empirical validation, latent factor modeling (EFA/CFA), and structured productive friction in S-SPARC, this study establishes a defensible paradigm to diagnose, quantify, and remediate cognitive atrophy in higher computer science education.

Problem Formulation
Classroom Reality
The Passive Behavioral Feedback Loop

Students routinely bypass mental reasoning through an automated copy-paste loop:

Copy Problem Statement → Prompt AI → Copy Generated Code → Submit to Auto-Grader

This cycle produces artificial success on automated unit test cases while masking critical cognitive deficits that lead to severe failure during unassisted, closed-book evaluations.

Result: High lab assignment scores mask complete inability to code independently.
Empirical Literature
Evidence from Peer-Reviewed Research
  • Critical Thinking Degradation: Systematic review shows excessive reliance on conversational AI impairs analytical reasoning, problem decomposition, and self-debugging [2].
  • Lack of Cognitive Transfer: Instant AI code generation exhibits near-zero correlation with independent conceptual mastery [3], [14].
  • Negative Final Grade Impact: High frequency of LLM dependence correlates negatively with summative exam scores [4].
  • Surface-Level Regulation: Analysis of 2,376 ChatGPT interactions proves students rarely engage in metacognitive reflection [5].
Literature Consensus: AI assistance without structured friction damages skill acquisition.
Pedagogical Diagnosis: The Mechanism of Cognitive Atrophy

Cognitive offloading eliminates the essential struggle of mental trace execution, edge-case debugging, and syntax structuring. When learners no longer grapple with syntax and logic errors independently, their foundational mental schema for algorithmic problem-solving atrophies [2], [6].

Mathematical Modeling & Latent Metrics
AI Over-Reliance Index (ORI): Mathematical Formulation
Mathematical Model
ORI = 1 - (w1 · Q_prompt + w2 · S_CIOE + w3 · R_concept + w4 · F_reflect)
All component indicators normalized to [0, 1] • Total weight constraint: w1 + w2 + w3 + w4 = 1.0
Indicator 1
Q_prompt (w1 = 0.30)

Shannon Information Entropy

H(X) = -∑ p(x) log2 p(x) [11]. Measures lexical richness and technical syntax token density within coding prompts [13], [17].

Penalizes generic, low-effort queries like "fix code".
Indicator 2
S_CIOE (w2 = 0.25)

Context Completeness

Measures compliance across 4 structured dimensions: Context, Input specs, Expected Output, and Error Trace [12].

Rewards rigorous problem specification.
Indicator 3
R_concept (w3 = 0.20)

Conceptual Ratio

Ratio of conceptual/logic inquiries versus requests for direct code generation (Bloom Levels C2-C4 vs C1).

Differentiates learning from direct outsourcing.
Indicator 4
F_reflect (w4 = 0.25)

Friction Adherence

Adherence to the 60s reflection window [9] coupled with keystroke cadence analysis to detect external copy-paste.

Prevents meta-gaming via external LLMs.

Psychometric Rigor Disclosure: The component weights above represent an exploratory baseline. Structural dimensionality and final weights are empirically calibrated via Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) and Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) across distinct student cohorts.

Scientific Contributions & Novelties
Four Pillars of Scientific Innovation
Original Contributions
Contribution I
Real-Time Composite Metric for Adaptive Scaffolding

Unlike post-hoc reliance taxonomies [15] or eye-tracking setups [16] analyzed after lab sessions, S-SPARC computes ORI scores dynamically during active coding. This enables the platform to throttle AI assistance dynamically and activate Socratic scaffolding when over-reliance is detected.

Impact: First real-time pedagogical intervention engine for Generative AI in CS.
Contribution II
Information-Theoretic Evaluation of Coding Prompts

Adapts Shannon Information Entropy H(X) [11] to quantify lexical diversity, structural complexity, and technical token density in student programming prompts, advancing significantly beyond naive character-count or word-length metrics [13], [17].

Impact: Objective mathematical quantification of prompt engineering depth.
Contribution III
Empirical Productive Friction & Anti Meta-Gaming

Empirically tests the pedagogical efficacy of mandatory 60-second reflection pauses [9], [10] reinforced by keystroke dwell-time analytics to verify human typing cadence and prevent students from bypassing the intervention via external LLMs.

Impact: Enforces authentic cognitive engagement rather than superficial compliance.
Contribution IV
Closed-Loop AI Tutor & LMS Auto-Grader Integration

Directly correlates continuous AI conversational telemetry with deterministic unit-test outcomes on E-STRANGE (pass rates, execution runtime, debugging iterations) and unassisted exam performance to establish learning transfer.

Impact: Connects AI dialog quality directly to measurable code execution rigor.

Research Ethics & Data Governance
Data Privacy & Mitigation of Pygmalion / Labeling Bias
Research Ethics
Pillar 1
Informed Consent & Opt-Out

Students receive full transparency regarding aggregated data analytics for curriculum improvement, with guaranteed penalty-free opt-out options that do not affect academic grading.

Pillar 2
De-Identification & Encryption

All personal identifiable information (Student ID, Name) is cryptographically hashed into unique random research identifiers prior to analysis. Datasets are stored on secure, access-controlled institutional servers.

Pillar 3
Preventing Pygmalion Effect

Individual ORI scores and behavioral personas are strictly blind-masked from course instructors during the semester to prevent algorithmic labeling from biasing subjective grading or teaching behavior.

Instructor Dashboard Harmonization: Phase-Gated Deployment

The live instructor evaluation dashboard marketed in the S-SPARC product roadmap follows a phase-gated deployment: during Phase 1 (Validation Pilot), individual metrics remain blind-masked to protect internal validity. Full live dashboard access is unlocked in Phase 2 once psychometric and construct validity are rigorously established.

Methodological Rigor & Psychometrics
Mitigating Reactivity, Confounders, and Estimation Biases
Methodological Rigor
Mitigation 1
Reactivity & Maturation Confound Mitigation
  • A-B Phase Design: Modules 1-2 serve as an unconstrained baseline phase to record natural traits; Modules 3-4 introduce active productive friction (60s pause).
  • Confound Disclosure: For a single-cohort design, maturation and friction effects are formally disclosed as a limitation; if ≥ 2 parallel sections are available, counterbalancing (A-B vs B-A) is implemented.
Mitigation 2
Two-Phase Psychometric Validation Roadmap
  • Phase 1 (Semester 1 / Cohort 1): Instrument development and Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) to discover latent factor loadings across the 4 ORI indicators.
  • Phase 2 (Semester 2 / Cohort 2): Independent Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) to verify construct unidimensionality and prevent model overfitting.
Mitigation 3
Entropy Inversion Paradox (Interaction Modeling)
  • Interaction Model: Formulates an Entropy × Baseline Prior Ability (pre-test / GPA) interaction term to ensure expert prompt conciseness is not misclassified as novice over-reliance.
Mitigation 4
NLP Classifier Sub-Study Validation (R_concept)
  • Double Annotation Protocol: Validates intent classification against ≥ 100 expert-annotated prompts by 2 independent raters, requiring Cohen's Kappa κ ≥ 0.80 before formula deployment.

Statistical Integrity & Open Science
Anti-Circularity Validation, Cross-Validation, & Pre-Registration
Statistical Integrity
Protocol 1
Anti-Circularity Cross-Validation

Eliminates circular validation by strictly separating regression weight calibration from validation testing using a rigorous 5-Fold Cross-Validation protocol.

Protocol 2
Standardized Convergent Validity

Cross-validates ORI scores against an internationally validated instrument: the GenAI Reliance Scale (Hou et al., Computers & Education 2025 [1]).

Protocol 3
OSF Pre-Registration Protocol

Locks the confirmatory analysis plan on the Open Science Framework (OSF.io) prior to data collection, eliminating p-hacking and researcher degrees of freedom.

Multicollinearity Diagnostics (Variance Inflation Factor • VIF < 5.0)

All predictor variables in the ORI formulation are evaluated for multicollinearity to ensure component orthogonality, parameter stability, and unambiguous interpretability of calibrated regression weights.

Secondary Exploratory Analyses
Four Exploratory Hypotheses with Rigorous Statistical Controls
Bonferroni Adjusted α = 0.0125
Hypothesis 1
H1. Volume-Driven Green AI Paradox

Hypothesis: Over-reliant students generate higher cumulative carbon emissions due to excessive prompt iteration volumes rather than per-query complexity.

Control: Controls for Module Difficulty Fixed-Effects and isolates query volume from semantic cache hit rates.

Hypothesis 2
H2. AST Code Distance Delta vs Variable Renaming

Hypothesis: Autonomous students exhibit significantly larger Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) structural distances between AI suggestions and final submissions.

Control: Evaluates the final AI-suggested code block within the session against the student's submitted code.

Hypothesis 3
H3. Reflexive Pause vs First-Attempt Pass Rate

Hypothesis: Active adherence to reflection pauses positively predicts first-attempt pass rates on auto-graded programming assignments.

Control: Incorporates baseline prior ability as a covariate; claims remain strictly associative rather than deterministic.

Hypothesis 4
H4. Query Novelty & Semantic Cache Lifecycle

Hypothesis: Semantic cache misses reflect deeper student exploratory inquiry into complex edge cases and non-standard problem boundaries.

Control: Controls for cache maturity lifecycle and assignment timing to prevent cold-start artifacts.

Methodological Scope
Boundary of Generalization

The ORI metric is specifically formulated for code-based problem-solving environments evaluable via deterministic unit testing within an automated grading framework.

Generalization to non-computing disciplines (e.g., qualitative essay composition or social sciences) requires non-deterministic evaluation rubrics and is formally defined as future work.
